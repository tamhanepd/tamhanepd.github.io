title: "Cavity-driven Gas Transport in Cool-Core Clusters: Implications for Multiphase Gas Cooling"
collection: publications
permalink: /publication/2026-09-paper1
excerpt: 'In this work we show that AGN-driven uplift appears to play an important role in regulating the formation and accumulation of cold molecular gas in cool-core galaxy clusters. Across 24 clusters, the molecular gas reservoir broadly scales with the amount of low-entropy gas that can be displaced by AGN cavities, supporting a picture in which repeated cycles of uplift and cooling help build the cold gas reservoirs of brightest cluster galaxies. Gas with cooling times of a few $10^8$ yr can be transported to the 10–30 kpc scales of Hα filaments, while gas with longer cooling times requires uplift to substantially larger radii. Several outliers highlight the importance of a cluster's dynamical history. MS0735 and the outer cavities of Hydra-A show displaced gas mass from larger cavities does not correlate with the observed molecular gas mass. A2029 has thermodynamic signatures suggestive of multiphase gas but no cold gas and cavities, while Zw2701 (MS1455.0+2232) has a substantial cold gas reservoir and similar thermodynamic indicators despite lacking obvious cavities or jets. These systems demonstrate that the thermodynamic state of the intracluster medium alone cannot explain the presence of cold gas. The history of AGN activity, gas uplift, and cooling also plays a crucial role.'
